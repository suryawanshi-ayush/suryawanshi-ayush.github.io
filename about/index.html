    Thesis
    Formic acid can power small fuel cells, but the platinum catalyst that oxidises it gets clogged by carbon monoxide from a side reaction, and the current fades within an hour. With Prof. Raghuram Chetty I tested two remedies: a sub-monolayer of bismuth on commercial Pt/C, which is thought to leave fewer of the neighbouring platinum sites that CO needs to form, and a dissolved quinone (AQSA) that acts as a redox mediator. In three-electrode half-cell tests the catalyst with the least bismuth worked best, keeping 92.9% of its current over a one-hour hold against 58.5% for unmodified Pt/C. Read more

What I enjoy working on

Problems where the chemistry and the engineering have to be solved together: how a catalyst behaves once it becomes an electrode, how a lab result changes on the way to a plant, and what a process really costs, stage by stage. I’m open to work in catalysis, electrochemistry or process engineering, on its own or where they meet.

My device-level experience so far is in three-electrode half-cells; full fuel cells and electrolysers are interests rather than work I’ve done.
