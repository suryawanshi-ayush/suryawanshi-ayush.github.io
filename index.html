How my work connects

From catalyst powder to process decisions

Most of my work has followed one thread: make a catalyst, turn it into an electrode, measure what it really does, and think about what it would take to use it. Each stage links to the project behind it.

    • SNCR retrofit design

Featured · M.Tech thesis, 2025–26

Keeping platinum clean in formic acid fuel cells

Platinum oxidises formic acid well until carbon monoxide blocks its surface. I modified commercial Pt/C with bismuth, added a quinone mediator, and tested which combination held its current longest.

current kept after a one-hour hold at 0.55 V vs RHE by Bi(1.25)-Pt/C, the catalyst with the least bismuth
92.9%
commercial Pt/C in the same test
58.5%

Three-electrode half-cell results in acid. They rank catalysts against each other; full-cell testing is the next step.

Now

Effluent treatment at LOXIM Industries

Since July 2026 I’ve been a Management Trainee in Ahmedabad, leading technology selection for sulphate removal from plant effluent. I evaluated electrocoagulation and electroflocculation as pretreatment and run screening, sample trials and feasibility checks with more than ten vendors.

The options shortlisted so far point to a projected 50–60% lower operating cost, from INR 2.5/L to about INR 1.0–1.2/L. The selection is still in progress.

What interests me

From half-cells to working devices

The questions I keep coming back to sit where electrochemistry meets process engineering: how a catalyst that looks good in a half-cell behaves in a membrane electrode assembly or a full electrolyser, how it ages over hundreds of hours, and what it takes to run it at plant scale. AEM electrolysis, fuel cells and green hydrogen are where those questions are most alive.

These are interests, not work I have done yet; my device-level experience so far is in three-electrode half-cells.
