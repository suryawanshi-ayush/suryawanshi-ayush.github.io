At a glance

Problem
Oxygen reduction limits fuel cell cathodes. How do its intermediates sit on a small Pt cluster, and is each step favourable in electronic energy?
My contribution
Individual course project: built, optimised and checked the Pt13 structures and the adsorbed intermediates in Gaussian 16 and tabulated the step energies.
Key result
All four tabulated step energies are negative (−1.85 to −3.90 eV, electronic energies), and all optimised structures are true minima.
Limits
Gas-phase electronic energies only: no zero-point, thermal or entropy corrections, so not free energies, and no transition states, so no barriers, rates or activity predictions.

Problem and baseline

Oxygen reduction is slow even on platinum, and it is the reaction that limits fuel cell cathodes. Small clusters like Pt13 have edge and corner sites that behave differently from flat surfaces. The question was how the reaction intermediates sit on such a cluster and whether each step of the associative four-electron pathway is favourable in electronic energy.

Baseline. The reference point is O2 adsorbed on the cluster (energy set to zero). Platinum is the benchmark catalyst for this reaction, so the project asks what a small platinum cluster looks like at each stage, not whether it beats another material.

My contribution

This was an individual project for the course Computational Methods in Catalysis at IIT Madras, guided by Dr. Jithin John Varghese. I built the structures, set up and ran the Gaussian 16 calculations, checked every structure, and tabulated and interpreted the step energies.

Methods and assumptions

  1. Built the structures

    The Pt13 cluster and the O2, OOH, O, OH and H2O species were built in Avogadro, each placed in a likely binding configuration.

  2. Optimised them

    Gaussian 16, spin-unrestricted B3LYP, LANL2DZ effective core potential for platinum and 6-31G(d,p) for hydrogen and oxygen. Route: opt=calcall freq ub3lyp/genecp scf=(xqc,maxcycle=512) guess=mix int=ultrafine.

  3. Checked them

    A frequency calculation on every structure confirmed it was a true minimum, with no imaginary frequencies.

  4. Compared the steps

    Reaction energies for each step of the associative pathway, from the electronic (SCF) energies of the optimised structures.

Environment
Gas phase, no explicit solvation.
Energies
SCF electronic energies. Zero-point, thermal and entropy corrections were available from the frequency calculations but not applied.
Configurations
One binding configuration per species is described; a search over adsorption sites or cluster isomers is not reported.
Transition states
Not located, so the pathway has no activation energies.

Results

Tabulated step energies (ΔE, electronic energy, eV)
StepReactionΔE (eV)
1O2 adsorption on Pt13 (reference)0
2OOH* formation−2.84
3O* and water formation−2.05
4OH* formation−1.85
5Water released, Pt13 regenerated−3.90

Every tabulated step energy is negative, which fits the established role of platinum as the benchmark oxygen reduction catalyst and supports using small clusters for mechanistic questions. That is as far as these numbers go.

Limitations

  • Not free energies. Only electronic energies are used, so the values are qualitative trends in relative stability. Zero-point, thermal and entropy terms matter, especially for gas-phase O2 and H2O.
  • No barriers, no rates. Without transition states the calculations say which intermediates are stable, not how fast any step goes, which step limits the reaction, or how active the cluster is. They are not an activity or rate prediction.
  • Idealised model. A single isolated Pt13 cluster in the gas phase, with no support, solvent or electrode environment.
  • Reading the figure. The original report draws the four tabulated values as points on one smoothed curve. Per-step energies are not cumulative energies, so the curve is a qualitative sketch, and its bends should not be read as barriers.

What I took from it. A calculation is only as strong as what it includes. Being explicit about what was left out, here free-energy corrections and transition states, is part of reporting it.
