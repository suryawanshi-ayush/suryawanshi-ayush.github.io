Oxygen reduction on a 13-atom platinum cluster

A DFT study of the four-electron oxygen reduction pathway on Pt13.

At a glance

Problem
Oxygen reduction limits fuel cell cathodes. How do its intermediates sit on a small Pt cluster?
What I did
Optimised O2, OOH*, O*, OH* and H2O on Pt13 and checked each was a true minimum.
Key result
Every step of the associative four-electron pathway was downhill in electronic energy.
Limits
Electronic energies only, no corrections or transition states, so no rates.

The question

Oxygen reduction is slow even on platinum, and it is the reaction that limits fuel cell cathodes. Small clusters like Pt13 have edge and corner sites that behave differently from flat surfaces. I wanted to see how the reaction intermediates sit on such a cluster and whether each step is energetically favourable.

What I did

  1. Built the structures

    The Pt13 cluster and the O2, OOH, O, OH and H2O species were built in Avogadro.

  2. Optimised them

    Gaussian 16 with the spin-unrestricted B3LYP functional, LANL2DZ for platinum and 6-31G(d,p) for hydrogen and oxygen.

  3. Checked them

    A frequency calculation on every structure confirmed it was a true minimum, with no imaginary frequencies.

  4. Compared the steps

    Reaction energies for each step of the associative four-electron pathway, from the electronic (SCF) energies.

What I found

All four steps were downhill, which fits the known activity of platinum for this reaction and supports using small clusters for mechanistic work.

See the optimised structures

Limits

These are electronic energies only, without zero-point, thermal or entropy corrections, and no transition states were located. They show which intermediates are stable, not how fast each step goes.
