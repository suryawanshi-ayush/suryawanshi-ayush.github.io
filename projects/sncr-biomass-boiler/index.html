At a glance

Problem
An upgraded biomass boiler must stay under 300 mg/Nm³ NOx. Could an SNCR retrofit get it there?
What I did
As one of four, estimated baseline NOx, checked the operating window, and modelled combustion and SNCR chemistry in Cantera.
Key result
About 60% reduction, bringing NOx to roughly 95–120 mg/Nm³ in the model.
Limits
A design study built on incomplete plant data, not a plant trial.

The question

An existing biomass boiler, run for district heating by Vardar Varme, is to be upgraded to 30–35 MW to supply steam to Billerud Viken’s planned bleached chemi-thermomechanical pulp (BCTMP) mill. Burning biomass produces NOx, and the plant has to stay under 300 mg/Nm³. Our team of four looked at whether a selective non-catalytic reduction (SNCR) retrofit could get it there.

How we approached it

  1. Estimated the starting point

    Uncontrolled NOx came out at 280–330 mg/Nm³, in the range where SNCR is effective.

  2. Checked the operating window

    Mass and energy balances gave a flame temperature near 1250 °C and a flue-gas residence time of 0.6–2 s, both suitable for SNCR.

  3. Modelled the chemistry

    A combustion equilibrium model and a plug-flow SNCR kinetic model in Python with Cantera, using the GRI-Mech 3.0 and San Diego mechanisms.

  4. Designed the system

    Ammonia was chosen as the reagent for cost and performance. We laid out storage, mixing and injection, sized the main equipment, set out ammonia safety measures and estimated capital and operating costs.

What we found

A realistic reduction of about 60% brings emissions to roughly 95–120 mg/Nm³, well under the limit. Because both performance and ammonia slip depend strongly on temperature, the study recommends injecting towards the upper end of the temperature window. Reagent use dominates the running cost.

I presented the work as a poster at Treforedlingsforum 2025 in Oslo on 18–19 November 2025.

What I took from it

This was my first design project for an industry client. It taught me to work from incomplete plant data, state assumptions clearly, and give a recommendation a client can act on.
