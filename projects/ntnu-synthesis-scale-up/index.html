Reworking a catalyst synthesis after an ignition on the filter

Three batches of Bi-modified Pt/C: a low-yield first batch, an ignition in the second, and a changed aqueous route with no ignition observed.

Type
Experimental research · KinCat group, NTNU
When
Aug – Dec 2025
Where
KinCat group, Department of Chemical Engineering, NTNU, Trondheim
Supervisor at NTNU
Prof. Edd Anders Blekkan
Funding
UTFORSK mobility scholarship
Methods

At a glance

Problem
Following the literature procedure recovered very little catalyst, and the scaled-up second batch ignited on the filter.
My contribution
Ran the three batches and changed the route after each one: an aqueous borohydride step, less reductant and a changed filtration.
Key result
No ignition was observed in Batch 3, which also gave the highest recovered mass, about 220 mg, with uniform SEM morphology.
Limits
Three batches, with a mass reported only for Batch 3. This does not validate scale-up, and no activity data are reported for the individual batches.

Problem and baseline

The thesis needed one synthesis route I could rely on. The baseline was the literature procedure for Bi-modified Pt/C, which uses a dichloromethane-based reduction. Followed exactly, it recovered very little catalyst: most of the material was lost during filtration, and the surface looked contaminated under the microscope.

My contribution

I ran all three batches in the KinCat group at NTNU, hosted by Prof. Edd Anders Blekkan, and changed the procedure after each one: first scaling up, then replacing the solvent-based reduction with an aqueous borohydride step, using less reductant and changing the filtration. I also took the SEM images of the batches.

Methods and assumptions

  • Comparison basis. Batches were compared on recovered mass, what happened during filtration, and SEM morphology. Electrochemical activity was not measured on the individual batches.
  • Safety observation. The ignition was seen during filtration, when residual sodium borohydride on the filter ignited. The report does not quantify residual borohydride or describe a formal hazard assessment.
  • Final route. Aqueous borohydride in place of the dichloromethane-based reduction, a smaller amount of reductant, and modified filtration conditions.

Results

Batch comparison
BatchRouteRecoverySafety observationSEM morphology
1Literature procedure, dichloromethane-based reductionExtremely low, from filtration losses and possible contamination during recoveryNo ignition reportedHeterogeneous surface, sheet-like features
2Scaled up from Batch 1More material than Batch 1 (mass not reported)Residual NaBH4 ignited during filtrationCleaner, uniform nanoparticle distribution
3Aqueous NaBH4, less reductant, modified filtrationHighest of the three, about 220 mgNo ignition observedUniform dispersion, dense raspberry-like nanoclusters

Batch sizes and recovered masses for Batches 1 and 2 are not given in the source report, so the table has no numbers for them.

SEM of Batches 2 and 3 showed densely packed nanoclusters with a rough surface and metal particles spread evenly over the carbon support, without the irregular, sheet-like features of Batch 1.

Limitations

  • Not a scale-up validation. About 220 mg from a single batch shows the changed route recovered more material and ran without an ignition at this size. It does not show that the route is safe or reproducible at larger scale.
  • “No ignition observed” is not “no risk”. Three batches were run and no hazard assessment was reported, so the observation is not proof that the risk is gone.
  • No activity data per batch. The comparison rests on recovered mass and microscopy.

What I took from it. Safety problems can sit in the handling steps rather than in the reaction itself, and a route that works at a small size needs to be checked again when it changes.
