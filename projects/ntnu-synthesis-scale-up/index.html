Making the catalyst synthesis safe to scale

Three batches of Bi-modified Pt/C, one ignition on the filter, and a cleaner aqueous route.

At a glance

Problem
The literature procedure lost most of the catalyst during filtration, and scaling it up made it unsafe.
What I did
Ran three batches and changed the route after each one.
Key result
An aqueous borohydride step with less reductant removed the ignition risk and gave the highest yield, about 220 mg.
Limits
Yield and morphology were the checks here; activity was tested on the thesis series.

The problem

The thesis needed one synthesis route I could rely on, and the first route I tried did not give it. I ran the Bi-modified Pt/C synthesis in three batches in the KinCat group at NTNU and changed it after each one.

Batch by batch

  1. Batch 1: following the paper

    The literature procedure recovered very little catalyst. Most of the loss happened during filtration, and SEM showed irregular, sheet-like features that pointed to contamination during recovery.

  2. Batch 2: scaling up

    A larger batch raised the yield, but leftover sodium borohydride ignited on the filter.

  3. Batch 3: changing the route

    I replaced the solvent-based reduction with an aqueous borohydride step, used less reductant and changed the filtration. There was no ignition, and the batch gave the highest recovered mass, about 220 mg.

What the material looked like

SEM of the later batches showed densely packed nanoclusters with a rough, raspberry-like surface and metal particles spread evenly over the carbon support, without the irregular features of the first batch.

What I took from it

Procedures that work at milligram scale can behave differently when scaled, and safety problems often show up in the handling steps rather than the reaction itself. The thesis catalyst series was made with the fully aqueous route.
