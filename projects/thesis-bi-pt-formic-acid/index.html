The question

Platinum oxidises formic acid well at first, but a side pathway leaves adsorbed carbon monoxide (CO) on the surface and the current decays. Modifying platinum with small amounts of bismuth has been reported to reduce this poisoning.

The thesis asked how the amount of bismuth on commercial Pt/C trades peak activity against how well the current holds up over an hour, and how a dissolved quinone mediator, anthraquinone-2-sulfonic acid (AQSA), changes that picture. Unmodified commercial Pt/C was the reference throughout.

My contribution and approach

This was my M.Tech thesis at IIT Madras, supervised by Prof. Raghuram Chetty, with the catalyst synthesis carried out during my research stay at NTNU. I did the experimental work and the analysis:

  • Made a series of catalysts: commercial Pt/C modified with bismuth at four loadings by a wet-chemical route.
  • Characterised them with electron microscopy and element mapping (FE-SEM, EDX), X-ray diffraction and, for the best catalyst, surface-area measurement.
  • Optimised the catalyst ink before the main comparisons.
  • Tested activity and durability in a three-electrode half-cell: cyclic voltammetry, and a one-hour constant-potential hold with and without AQSA.

Key findings

1. Activity and retention pulled in different directions

The lowest-bismuth catalyst, Bi(1.25)-Pt/C, had the highest one-hour current retention in both electrolytes, and the highest loading had the lowest without AQSA. Activity ranked differently: Bi(5)-Pt/C had the highest forward-scan Peak 2 mass activity of the series (851.7 against 596.7 mA mg−1Pt for Bi(1.25)-Pt/C), which the thesis assigns largely to oxidative removal of CO that had already adsorbed rather than to the direct pathway. It retained less current over the hour.

Retention is not output. Because it started higher, Bi(5)-Pt/C still had the highest absolute current density at the end of the hold without AQSA; Bi(1.25)-Pt/C ended only slightly above commercial Pt/C. Its advantage is slower decay.

The brief rise above 100% for Bi(1.25)-Pt/C early in the hold is attributed in the thesis to bismuth rearranging on the surface; that is an interpretation, not a measurement.

2. AQSA lowered one-hour current retention in these tests

With AQSA in the electrolyte, one-hour retention was lower for all five catalysts than without it, and Bi(1.25)-Pt/C remained the most durable. This result concerns one-hour retention at the single AQSA concentration used in the durability comparison; it is not a general verdict on the mediator.

3. Supporting characterisation and ink optimisation

At the two lowest loadings, electron microscopy showed no separate bismuth crystallites and X-ray diffraction resolved no distinct crystalline bismuth reflections, consistent with a thin bismuth decoration on the platinum. At the highest loading, bismuth-rich agglomerates appeared in SEM and EDX. These observations support, but do not prove, the proposed surface-decoration model.

X-ray diffraction patterns

FCC platinum reflections remain visible across the series, weakest at the highest loading, and no distinct crystalline bismuth reflections are resolved at the two lowest loadings. The highest loading shows additional sharp reflections, which the thesis tentatively attributes to a bismuth-containing phase.

Optimising the ink mattered too. A thinner Nafion binder film gave about 2.5–2.8 times the forward-scan peak current of a thicker one, on commercial Pt/C and on a Bi-modified catalyst, even though on Pt/C the thicker film showed more electrochemically active area. The thinner ink was used for all later comparisons.

Evidence and study limits

This page shows selected thesis figures and summary results. Detailed recipes, operating parameters, compositions and raw data are not included.

  • Half-cell, not a fuel cell. All results come from three-electrode half-cell tests in acid at room temperature. They rank catalysts against each other; a membrane electrode assembly and single-cell tests are the next step named in the thesis.
  • One hour. Retention was measured over a one-hour hold; practical devices run for hundreds to thousands of hours.
  • No repeatability data. The thesis reports no replicate counts, standard deviations or error bars, so the differences are indicative rankings, not statistically tested ones.
  • Coarse loading grid. Four bismuth loadings were tested; the tested loading grid was too coarse to locate the optimum.
  • Inferred mechanism. Site blocking by bismuth is a proposed mechanism that the results are consistent with; it was not observed directly, and the illustration above is conceptual.

What I took from it. A good-looking voltammogram is not the same as a durable catalyst, and retention is not the same as output. I now check durability before ranking anything, and I ask what each measurement can and cannot see.
