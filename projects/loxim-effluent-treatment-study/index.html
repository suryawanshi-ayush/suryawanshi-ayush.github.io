At a glance

Problem
A high-sulphate dye effluent had to be treated at a running cost, and its sulphate left the plant as a waste salt. Where does that cost go, and can the salt become a product?

The question

An effluent from dye production carried a high load of sulphate. Treating it had a running cost, and the sulphate ended up as a waste salt. The study asked where that cost actually goes, which treatment routes could lower it, and whether the salt could leave the plant as a product instead of a waste.

My contribution and approach

I led the study during my first months as a Management Trainee at LOXIM Industries.

  1. Mapped the cost

    Built a baseline of the current treatment, stage by stage, to see which steps drive the operating cost.

  2. Screened the routes

    Compared electrochemical, membrane and crystallisation routes, including electrocoagulation and electroflocculation as pretreatment, through screening, sample trials and feasibility checks with more than ten technology vendors.

  3. Built the train

    Combined the shortlisted steps into one treatment train and simulated it in DWSIM.

  4. Costed it

    Modelled the operating cost of each stage and compared the train with the current treatment on the same basis.

The proposed treatment train

Result

The proposed train came out at about two-thirds lower modelled operating cost than the current treatment, and it turns the sulphate into a saleable salt rather than a waste. The study was handed over with a validation roadmap, so the next stage of evaluation can test these numbers at bench scale.

What earlier work brought to it

  • Electrochemistry. Electrocoagulation and electroflocculation run on electrodes and applied current, so my half-cell and electrode work helped in judging them. Thesis
  • Process economics. The SNCR design study also chose a route on performance and cost and estimated running costs. SNCR study
  • Reading claims against data. Vendor claims were checked against what each trial could actually show, the same habit as stating the limits of a half-cell result.

Limits and what is not shown

  • A modelled saving. The figure comes from a stage-wise operating-cost model, covers quantified items only, and has not been validated at bench or plant scale.
  • Handed over. This was a short-term study. The proposal and its validation roadmap were passed on for further evaluation; no plant decision is claimed here.
  • Company property. The effluent analysis, supplier names, absolute costs and the study report belong to LOXIM Industries and are not shown.

What I took from it. In a plant, the best route is the one whose cost you can explain stage by stage. Mapping the cost first made the choice of technologies much clearer than comparing vendors' headline claims.
